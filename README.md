# Energetic constraints reshape climate-tracking opportunities for large mammals across European protected-area networks

_Gavin Stark, Jeremy Dertien ,Nikolaj Rauff Poulsen, Emilio Berti, Andrés Camilo Mármol Guijarro, Magali Weissgerber, Néstor Fernández, Henrique M. Pereira_

### Please contact the lead author for questions about the code or data: Gavin Stark (gavinstark89@gmail.com)

### Study overview

Protected areas (PAs) remain central to biodiversity conservation, but climate change is shifting temperature conditions beyond the boundaries of many protected sites. Their effectiveness therefore increasingly depends on both the time for which analogous climatic conditions remain available within a PA and the potential for species to move through surrounding landscapes. Our study develops a climate-informed connectivity framework for 10,908 terrestrial PAs across Europe. It integrates temperature-based climate velocity, protected-area climatic residence time, current habitat suitability, physical and anthropogenic barriers, and terrain-related energetic movement costs. Functional connectivity is evaluated for ten focal large-mammal taxa, summarized into two analytical archetypes: large ungulates (the herbivore archetype) and large carnivores (the carnivore archetype). The framework identifies where climatic persistence and movement potential coincide, where they are spatially mismatched, and where climate-tracking opportunities may be constrained by both rapid climatic turnover and low landscape permeability.

### Conservation relevance

The results support prioritizing strategic improvements in movement feasibility rather than attempting to maximize connectivity everywhere. Potential actions include maintaining permeable foothill and transitional landscapes, creating stepping stones, mitigating transport and border barriers, protecting channelized carnivore bottlenecks, and coordinating restoration across national boundaries. Different combinations require different responses: exposed and weakly connected PAs may need both internal buffering and external linkages; climatically persistent but isolated sites may require improved accessibility; and connected but climate-exposed sites require species-specific assessment of whether pathways reach suitable future conditions. This framework can support the development of the Trans-European Nature Network, national restoration strategies, transboundary corridor planning, and broader European biodiversity policy by identifying where targeted intervention could most effectively strengthen climate-tracking opportunities.
